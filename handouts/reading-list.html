Block 1 — What economic evaluation is

The policy decision we open with

  • Press Information Bureau, Government of India. Cervical Cancer Vaccination Campaign launched. 28 February 2026 (PRID 2234009). Single-dose quadrivalent HPV vaccine for girls aged 14, about 1.15 crore girls a year, free at government facilities, introduced into the national routine immunisation schedule with GAVI support. Burden figures cited from GLOBOCAN 2022: over 120,000 new cervical cancer cases and about 80,000 deaths a year in India, roughly 25% of global cervical cancer deaths.

Block 2 — Making decisions in health care

Where the threshold comes from

  • Chugh Y, Jyani G, Kar SS, Patro BK, Trivedi M, Albert S, Raman S, Rajsekar K, Prinja S; India cost-effectiveness threshold (CET) estimation study group. Estimating the willingness-to-pay per quality-adjusted life-year to aid health technology assessment in India. Value Health 2026;29(3):457–66. PMID 41176029 India's first empirical WTP per QALY. 5,460 respondents across six states, 21,640 observations, 12 hypothetical health states, time trade-off plus willingness to pay, gains discounted at 3%. Mean weighted ₹2,12,307 (US$2,535); range across health states ₹1,70,414 to ₹2,58,985; equivalent to 1.0 to 1.52 times GDP per capita. Read the limitations section, because this is a demand-side estimate.
  • Woods B, Revill P, Sculpher M, Claxton K. Country-level cost-effectiveness thresholds: initial estimates and the need for further research. Value Health 2016;19(8):929–35. PMID 27987642 The supply-side counterpart. Opportunity-cost-based thresholds of 1 to 51% of GDP per capita for the countries modelled. Conclusion: routinely used thresholds have been too high.
  • Bertram MY, Lauer JA, De Joncheere K, Edejer T, et al. Cost-effectiveness thresholds: pros and cons. Bull World Health Organ 2016;94(12):925–30. PMID 27994285 WHO-affiliated authors on why the 1 to 3 times GDP per capita rule should not be used as a decision rule.
  • Marseille E, Larson B, Kazi DS, Kahn JG. Thresholds for the cost-effectiveness of interventions: alternative approaches. Bull World Health Organ 2015;93(2):118–24. PMID 25883405

League tables

  • Shah K, Singh M, Kotwani P, Tyagi K, Pandya A, Saha S, Saxena D, Rajshekar K. Comprehensive league table of cost-utility ratios: a systematic review of cost-effectiveness evidence for health policy decisions in India. Front Public Health 2022;10:831254. PMID 36311623 79 studies, 2003 to 2019. 70% reported DALYs and 30% QALYs; 73% discounted at 3%, 4% at 5%, and 23% did not discount at all; sensitivity analysis in 73%, budget impact in only 18%. Read it for the methodological heterogeneity as much as for the table.

A published threshold, and what happens when it moves

  • NICE. Changes to NICE's cost-effectiveness thresholds confirmed. 1 December 2025. nice.org.uk £20,000 to £30,000 per QALY becomes £25,000 to £35,000 from April 2026. NICE estimates 3 to 5 additional medicines or indications a year. A new EQ-5D-5L value set was announced alongside.
  • Vallejo-Torres L, Edney LC, Espinosa O, Karnon J, Longo F. Politicised changes to the NICE threshold risk making cost-effectiveness analysis performative, not informative. Appl Health Econ Health Policy 2026;24(2):243–6. PMID 41513890 Short, sharp, and the best thing to hand anyone who thinks thresholds are a technical matter.

Health Res Policy Syst 2025;24(1):2. PMID 41316276 Background reading, not taught from a slide. 33 published HTAIn studies rated against Drummond's checklist and the Indian Reference Case; equity and heterogeneity fared worst. Recommends a formal topic-selection framework and identifying areas for disinvestment. The analysis covers studies published up to its own cut-off and HTAIn output has grown well beyond that since, so read the proportions as a snapshot rather than a current description.

Worked examples used in the slides

  • Chauhan AS, Prinja S, Srinivasan R, et al. Cost effectiveness of strategies for cervical cancer prevention in India. PLoS One 2020;15(9):e0238291. PMID 32870941 Markov model, societal perspective, lifetime horizon, primary Indian cost and quality-of-life data. The best single methods read in this list.
  • Gupta N, Verma RK, Gupta S, Prinja S. Cost effectiveness of trastuzumab for management of breast cancer in India. JCO Glob Oncol 2020;6:205–16. PMID 32045547 The uncertainty example. 1.29 QALYs at ₹1,78,877 per QALY with a 4% probability of being cost-effective using HERA estimates; 1.69 QALYs at ₹1,34,413 with 57.3% using the joint NSABP/NCCTG analysis; 90% with a 15 to 35% price cut.
  • Esposito DH, Tate JE, Kang G, Parashar UD. Projected impact and cost-effectiveness of a rotavirus vaccination program in India, 2008. Clin Infect Dis 2011;52(2):171–7. PMID 21288839 One-way sensitivity analysis in which vaccine price and efficacy against severe disease dominated everything else.
  • Chugh Y, Dhiman RK, Premkumar M, Prinja S, et al. Real-world cost-effectiveness of pan-genotypic sofosbuvir–velpatasvir versus genotype-dependent regimens for hepatitis C in Punjab's universal coverage scheme. PLoS One 2019;14(8):e0221769. PMID 31465503 A well-conducted state-level Indian cost-utility analysis, useful as a second appraisal paper.

If you read only three things

  1. Drummond ch. 1. Nineteen pages, and it settles the classification question for good.
  2. Sharma 2023, the Indian Reference Case. The standard your own work will be judged against, and short.
  3. Gupta 2020, trastuzumab. The clearest demonstration in the Indian literature of why a point-estimate ICER is not enough.

And if you read a fourth: Chauhan 2020 in PLoS One, an Indian cost-utility analysis, well conducted, on a decision that was actually taken. Read the methods, not just the abstract.
