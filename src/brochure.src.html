About the workshop

Health technology assessment now shapes what the Indian health system pays for, from the national immunisation schedule to the packages funded under PMJAY. The questions it answers come from clinicians and researchers, and the evidence it rests on is produced in institutes like this one. This one-day workshop is an introduction for anyone who wants to read that literature critically, judge whether a published cost-effectiveness claim deserves belief, and take part in producing it. It is built around the Indian Reference Case for health technology assessment, and every worked example is a published Indian study. Participants work in small groups on one real evaluation from their own field, carried through the whole day rather than read cold at the end. No prior training in health economics, modelling or statistics is required.

Workshop highlights

  • Eight published Indian evaluations, appraised in small groups across the day
  • A printed costing worksheet for a service in your own department
  • Six interactive widgets that run on a phone and stay online afterwards
  • Short live polls in every session, so nobody sits passively
  • The Indian Reference Case as a one-page appraisal form to take away
  • Every citation verified against PubMed, with a full reading list
  • Slides, handouts and widgets remain freely available online

Who should attend

  • Faculty, senior and junior residents, and research project staff, across departments
  • Anyone planning, or invited to co-investigate, a cost-effectiveness study
  • Researchers adding an economic component to a protocol or a grant application
  • Anyone who reads, reviews or is asked to comment on cost-effectiveness evidence
  • Departments considering submitting a topic to HTAIn through the Resource Centre
Prerequisites: None. No prior training in health economics, modelling or statistics is assumed. All exercises are paper-based.
